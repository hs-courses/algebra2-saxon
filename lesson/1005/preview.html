Lesson 1005: Modeling with Equations

To model a real situation with an equation:

  1. Identify the unknown quantity — assign it a variable.
  2. Identify a fixed starting amount and a rate of change (per unit).
  3. Write: total = fixed amount + rate × variable (or the inequality version with <, >, ≤, ≥).
  4. Solve, then interpret the answer in the context of the problem.

Worked Examples

A cab charges $3 plus $2 per mile. Write an equation for total cost C after m miles, then find the cost for 6 miles.

  1. C = 3 + 2m
  2. Substitute m=6: C = 3 + 2(6) = 3 + 12
  3. C = 15

A gym charges $20 to join plus $15/month. Write an inequality for total cost T being less than $95 after m months, then solve for m.

  1. T = 20 + 15m < 95
  2. Subtract 20: 15m < 75
  3. Divide by 15: m < 5

Lesson Practice

A plumber charges $50 plus $30/hour. Write an equation for total cost C after h hours.

Using C = 50 + 30h, find the cost for 4 hours.

A parking garage charges $5 plus $2/hour. Write an inequality for total cost T being at most $15 after h hours.

Solve 5 + 2h <= 15 for h.

A phone plan costs $25 plus $0.10 per text. Write an equation for total cost C after t texts.
